# Educator Guide
## Objectives
Distinguish theoretical probability from observed frequency; explain small-sample variation; compare sample sizes; reason about fairness using expected points.
## Prerequisites
Basic fractions/decimals and multiplication. Suggested session: 10–15 minutes.
## Activities
Coin Lab: predict, run 10/100/1000 trials, compare observed frequency with theory, and explore a biased probability.
Dice Investigation: compare one-die faces with two-dice sums and inspect the equivalent data table.
Fair-Game Designer: choose probabilities and non-monetary point rewards and compare expected points.
## Differentiation
Pairs can predict before running experiments. Start with smaller samples for support; compare repeated runs for extension.
## References
OpenStax, “Introductory Statistics 2e”: https://openstax.org/details/books/introductory-statistics-2e
NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook of Statistical Methods: https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/
These support probability concepts and interpretation of observed data.
## Model limitations
Browser randomness is an educational demonstration, not scientific random-number research. The game model uses expected points only.
## Offline follow-up
Flip a coin 10 times and 100 times, compare proportions, and explain why results need not match exactly.
